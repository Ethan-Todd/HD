## Why the methods disagree

The DCF reflects a five-year forecast of Home Depot's organic demand, acquisition contribution, margins, working capital, and reinvestment. Peer multiples import the market's pricing of Lowe's and a qualified but less comparable Tractor Supply; P/E values equity earnings, while EV/EBITDA values operations before the debt/cash bridge. The market price implies stronger growth than the base forecast: the reverse DCF needs a **+6.23 percentage-point** uniform shift to all five explicit growth rates with WACC, terminal growth, margins, reinvestment, debt, cash, and shares held fixed.

The **$177.82–$330.03** WACC–terminal-growth table is not the recommendation range. It shows model sensitivity at parameter corners; the narrower operating range comes from linked downside/base/upside business cases. The downside value of **$220.99** does not change the existing watch/defer action, but it means a price decline alone is not an entry signal if operating evidence is simultaneously worsening.
