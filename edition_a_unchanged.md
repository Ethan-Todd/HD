### Operating-value driver

I will initially use **comparable sales growth** as an operating-value driver because it helps separate performance at the established store and online base from growth produced by acquisitions and new stores. Fiscal 2025 comparable sales increased 0.3%. The filing says total net sales increased 3.2%, primarily because SRS and GMS contributed about $6.3 billion of incremental sales. This distinction matters because acquisition-driven revenue does not by itself prove stronger organic economics.

Average ticket is a supporting driver. Management identifies average ticket as a primary sales-performance driver. It increased 1.4% to $90.56, while comparable customer transactions fell 1.0%. This suggests that ticket growth, rather than transaction growth, supported comparable sales.

## Initial thesis

Home Depot has scale, brand recognition, broad distribution, and a profitable core business. Fiscal 2025 net sales grew to $164.7 billion, but much of the increase came from acquisitions. Meanwhile, operating margin declined to 12.7%, diluted EPS fell to $14.23, and comparable customer transactions declined. These results create uncertainty about the strength of organic demand and the incremental returns from recent acquisitions. The company may still be attractive at the right price, but I need a complete valuation and evidence that margins and organic sales can stabilize before initiating.

## Consequential assumptions

1. Comparable sales eventually grow at a modest positive rate rather than depending mainly on acquisitions.
2. Operating margin can stabilize near its recent range after acquisition integration and tariff pressures.
3. SRS and GMS produce adequate incremental cash flow and do not permanently dilute returns on invested capital.
4. Home Depot can refinance and service its debt without materially constraining reinvestment or dividends.
5. A normalized housing and remodeling environment supports long-run demand, although near-term timing is uncertain.
6. Diluted weighted-average shares are an appropriate starting share convention; the final valuation will reconcile this with period-end shares and expected dilution.

## Unknowns

- The market price and market capitalization on the valuation date.
- A defensible normalized revenue growth rate and operating margin.
- The appropriate beta, equity risk premium, cost of debt, and weighted-average cost of capital.
- The amount of debt and lease obligations that should be included in the final enterprise-to-equity bridge.
- The sustainable free-cash-flow contribution and integration costs of SRS and GMS.
- Whether current housing turnover, remodeling demand, tariffs, and interest rates represent temporary or lasting pressure.
- Home Depot's intrinsic value per diluted share and margin of safety at the current price.
