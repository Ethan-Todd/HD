# Home Depot and Partner Review — 10-Minute Notes

This is a short speaking guide for a roughly 10-minute partner exchange. It is for an AI finance class and is not professional financial advice.

## Three questions my partner can ask me

### 1. Why did you choose 2.5% revenue growth for Home Depot?

I used 2.5% as a moderate judgment based on recent reported growth of approximately negative 3.0%, positive 4.5%, and positive 3.2%. It assumes gradual growth without relying on a major housing recovery or continued acquisition growth every year.

### 2. What assumption had the biggest effect on your valuation?

Over my tested ranges, SG&A efficiency had the biggest effect. Moving SG&A from 56% to 54% of gross profit increased 2030 operating profit by $1,244.6 million, FCFE by $947.8 million, and value from $208.88 to $222.24 per share. This does not prove SG&A is always the most important driver because I tested it over a wider percentage-point range than gross margin.

### 3. What would change your valuation conclusion?

I would reconsider if Home Depot produced stronger comparable sales, kept gross margin above 33.4%, improved SG&A toward 54% of gross profit, or converted inventory into cash more efficiently. My classroom conclusion remains watch and defer because my FCFE value was $208.88, my FCFF value was $201.05, and the peer midpoint was about $240, compared with the saved $292.68 market price on September 24, 2026.

- Partner's conclusion, main driver, and biggest limitation: Revenue growth was his main driver; the biggest limitation was that the exact numerical result and source support were not available to verify during the discussion.
- Strength I identified: He connected revenue growth to higher operating performance rather than treating the changed share value as an unexplained output.
