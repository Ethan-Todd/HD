# Project 1 Lab 4 — Home Depot Acquisitions

**Company:** The Home Depot, Inc. (NYSE: HD)  
**Valuation date:** September 1, 2026  
**Decision:** Whether to initiate a position based on the question: **Will Home Depot's acquisitions pay off?**

## Thesis

**My call: watch/defer.** SRS and GMS added specialty products, distribution locations, and professional-customer relationships. ([FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1 and Note 13](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/354950/000162828026019436/hd-20260201.htm)) My judgment is that the disclosed results do not yet show whether those businesses earn more than Home Depot's cost of capital.

## Evidence

- Home Depot acquired SRS Distribution in June 2024 for approximately **$18.0 billion**. Management said the acquisition was intended to expand Home Depot's business with professional customers and specialty-trade distributors. ([FY2025 Form 10-K, Note 13, pp. 70–72](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/354950/000162828026019436/hd-20260201.htm))
- SRS acquired GMS on September 4, 2025. Home Depot paid approximately **$4.3 billion** for GMS equity and repaid approximately **$1.2 billion** of its debt. ([FY2025 Form 10-K, Note 13, pp. 70–72](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/354950/000162828026019436/hd-20260201.htm))
- GMS contributed **$2.0 billion of net sales** between the acquisition date and the end of fiscal 2025, but its attributable net earnings were described as **immaterial**. ([FY2025 Form 10-K, Note 13, pp. 70–72](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/354950/000162828026019436/hd-20260201.htm)) In my judgment, sales without material earnings are not enough to demonstrate value creation.
- At February 1, 2026, SRS, including GMS, operated more than **1,250 locations** in the United States and Canada. ([FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1, p. 3](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/354950/000162828026019436/hd-20260201.htm)) I view the larger network as a possible strategic advantage, but the filing does not assign a financial return to it.
- In the second quarter of fiscal 2026, consolidated sales increased **5.7%** and comparable sales increased **1.7%**. ([Home Depot Q2 FY2026 results, August 18, 2026](https://ir.homedepot.com/news-releases/2026/08-18-2026-110040463)) For the first six months, GAAP operating margin fell to **13.2% from 13.7%**. ([Home Depot Q2 FY2026 results, August 18, 2026](https://ir.homedepot.com/news-releases/2026/08-18-2026-110040463)) My reading is that sales improved before consolidated profitability did.
- Home Depot recorded **$244 million** of acquired-intangible amortization related to SRS and its subsidiaries during the first six months of fiscal 2026. ([Home Depot Q2 FY2026 results, August 18, 2026](https://ir.homedepot.com/news-releases/2026/08-18-2026-110040463)) I do not treat amortization alone as evidence that the acquisitions succeeded or failed.

## What management promised—and what happened

### Fiscal 2023 10-K and the fiscal 2024 outlook

Management expected fiscal 2024 total sales to grow about **1%**, comparable sales to decline about **1%**, operating margin to be about **14.1%**, and EPS to grow about **1%**. ([FY2024 guidance, February 20, 2024](https://ir.homedepot.com/news-releases/2024/02-20-2024-110037286)) Actual total sales rose **4.5%**, including contributions from SRS and a 53rd week, while comparable sales fell **1.8%**, operating margin was **13.5%**, and EPS fell about **1.3%**. ([FY2024 results, February 25, 2025](https://ir.homedepot.com/news-releases/2025/02-25-2025-110147741)) My score: total sales beat, but comparable sales, operating margin, and EPS missed the original targets.

### Fiscal 2024 10-K and the fiscal 2025 outlook

Management initially expected fiscal 2025 total sales to grow about **2.8%**, comparable sales to grow about **1%**, operating margin to be about **13.0%**, and EPS to decline about **3%**. ([Original FY2025 guidance, February 25, 2025](https://ir.homedepot.com/news-releases/2025/02-25-2025-110147741)) Actual sales rose **3.2%**, comparable sales increased **0.3%**, operating margin was **12.7%**, and EPS declined **4.6%**. ([FY2025 results, February 24, 2026](https://ir.homedepot.com/news-releases/2026/02-24-2026-110040985)) My score: total sales beat, but comparable sales, operating margin, and EPS missed the original targets.

### Acquisition promises

When Home Depot announced SRS, management said it would accelerate growth with residential professionals, add specialty-trade capabilities, and increase the addressable market by about **$50 billion**. ([SRS announcement, March 28, 2024](https://corporate.homedepot.com/news/company/home-depot-announces-agreement-acquire-srs-distribution-leading-specialty-trade)) SRS contributed **$6.4 billion** of sales during seven months of fiscal 2024, opened more than 20 locations, completed four tuck-in acquisitions, and entered nearly every QuoteCenter market for roofing products. ([Q4 FY2024 management call](https://ir.homedepot.com/~/media/Files/H/HomeDepot-IR/documents/hd-4q24-transcript.pdf)) Management later described SRS as exceeding expectations and producing revenue synergies. ([Q2 FY2025 management call](https://ir.homedepot.com/~/media/Files/H/HomeDepot-IR/reports-and-presentations/quarterly-earnings/2025/hd-2q25-transcript-vf.pdf)) I regard those as encouraging operating signs, but not proof of an adequate investment return because Home Depot does not separately report SRS free cash flow or acquisition ROIC. ([FY2025 Form 10-K, segment data and Note 13](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/354950/000162828026019436/hd-20260201.htm))

## Management interviews and recent news

In the Q2 FY2025 call, management said SRS was producing revenue synergies and market-leading growth. ([Q2 FY2025 call](https://ir.homedepot.com/~/media/Files/H/HomeDepot-IR/reports-and-presentations/quarterly-earnings/2025/hd-2q25-transcript-vf.pdf)) By the FY2025 year-end call, management reported cross-selling wins while also acknowledging weak roofing demand and pricing pressure. ([Q4 FY2025 call](https://ir.homedepot.com/~/media/Files/H/HomeDepot-IR/reports-and-presentations/quarterly-earnings/hd-4q25-transcript.pdf)) In Q2 FY2026, sales increased **5.7%**, comparable sales increased **1.7%**, and management maintained its annual guidance. ([Q2 FY2026 results](https://ir.homedepot.com/news-releases/2026/08-18-2026-110040463)) My interpretation is that the business is stabilizing, but the acquisition return remains unproven.

## What supports the strategy

SRS and GMS added roofing, landscaping, pool, drywall, ceiling, and steel-framing products plus a large professional distribution network. ([FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1 and Note 13](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/354950/000162828026019436/hd-20260201.htm)) My view is that this creates credible cross-selling and delivery opportunities, but an opportunity is not the same as a realized return.

## What weakens the case

Home Depot reports SRS within non-reportable operating segments and does not provide separate SRS or GMS free cash flow or acquisition ROIC. ([FY2025 Form 10-K, segment data and Note 13](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/354950/000162828026019436/hd-20260201.htm)) My judgment is that this prevents a reliable comparison of acquisition returns with the cost of capital.

Home Depot paid approximately **$18.0 billion** for SRS and approximately **$4.3 billion** for GMS equity while also repaying about **$1.2 billion** of GMS debt. ([FY2025 Form 10-K, Note 13, pp. 70–72](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/354950/000162828026019436/hd-20260201.htm)) In my view, that amount of committed capital makes weak margins, poor cash conversion, or failed integration financially important.

## Key assumptions and unknowns

For the acquisitions to create value, I believe their incremental after-tax cash flow must earn more than the return required on their purchase prices, assumed debt, integration costs, and working capital. The filings do not disclose enough acquisition-level data to complete that test. ([FY2025 Form 10-K, segment data and Note 13](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/354950/000162828026019436/hd-20260201.htm))

## What I would monitor

- Acquisition ROIC compared with WACC.
- SRS and GMS operating margins and free cash flow.
- Organic growth separated from acquisition growth.
- Customer retention, cross-selling, and delivery economics.
- Debt reduction and interest expense.
- Clearer management disclosure of acquisition results.

## Conclusion

**My call is watch/defer because the strategic progress is visible, but the financial return on the acquisition spending is not.**

**I would change the call to initiate if Home Depot disclosed sustained SRS/GMS cash returns above its cost of capital without worsening leverage.**
