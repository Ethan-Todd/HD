## Lease and bridge convention

Finance leases are financing debt and included once. Home Depot and Lowe's finance leases are already within reported debt; Tractor Supply presents finance leases separately, so they are added to debt. Operating leases are excluded from EV debt for all three because operating lease costs remain inside reported operating income and EBITDA; no company is lease-capitalized selectively.

EV = September 1 price × latest public actual shares + financing debt − cash. EBITDA = latest annual GAAP operating income + the D&A expense presented with operating expenses. This mixes a point-in-time market/balance-sheet numerator with latest annual trailing operating results and is not a forward multiple.

Applying the median P/E to HD's reported $14.23 EPS gives **$240.00 per share**. Applying the median EV/EBITDA to HD's $24,163m EBITDA gives **$270,038.4m enterprise value** and, after the same $0 excess-cash and $52,896m debt bridge used in the DCF, **$217.21 per diluted share**.

The methods disagree because P/E embeds interest, leverage, tax and the annual weighted-average share basis, while EV/EBITDA precedes D&A, tax and the bridge. Both depend heavily on a two-company peer set, and Tractor Supply is only qualified. They are comparison references—not values to average with each other or the DCF.
